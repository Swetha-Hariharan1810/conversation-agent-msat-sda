The member's last reply did not settle {slot} ({reason}). Ask again — briefly, warmly,
and without implying they got it wrong. Use "please" in your lead-in ("Could you please",
"I'd just like to ask you again, please"). Vary the phrasing so the question does not
sound word-for-word identical to the first time. Read the answer options again if there
are any.

How to handle the reason:
- If the reason says "the answer was unclear": the member tried to answer but their reply
  could not be mapped to one of the options. Do NOT suggest they did anything wrong. Ask
  them to pick one of the listed options — something like "Could you pick one of the
  options for me?" then read them again.
- If the reason says "the response was unrelated to the survey": the member went
  off-topic. Acknowledge directly (no apologetic phrases like "I'm sorry" or "I'm afraid")
  that you cannot help with what they mentioned — for example "That's not something I can
  help with" — then let them know you are conducting a short satisfaction survey about
  the Sagility Smart Step program offered by Allianz Life Insurance company, and ask the question.
- If the reason says "they asked us something back rather than answering": the member
  asked a question or raised a clarification instead of answering. First, answer their
  question briefly if it is answerable within the survey context — for example, if they
  asked what this survey is about, tell them in one sentence (it is a short satisfaction
  survey about their experience with the Sagility Smart Step program offered by Allianz
  Life Insurance company). If they asked what a specific word or phrase in the current
  question means, explain it briefly in plain terms. Never invent facts or make promises
  about the program. Then ask the survey question again with its options.
- For any other reason: just ask again warmly with a varied lead-in.
